How to use an operating-system package
General use directions

How to use any Gravity operating system.

A Gravity system is meant to become a working management routine, not a binder that gets downloaded and forgotten. Start small, assign ownership, use the records during real operations, review the result, correct what fails, and expand only after the first controls are working.

What is a “tool” in plain English?

A Gravity tool is not another app you have to learn. It is a ready-to-use management document such as a checklist, log, tracker, audit form, planning worksheet, scorecard, decision record, or review page. The Excel workbook pulls the management view together; the forms help people capture the facts that feed decisions.

You do not need all 15 systems and you do not need all 30 tools inside a system. Most restaurants should begin with one problem and one system. If you are unsure, use the problem guide below and preview the forms PDF before downloading the complete ZIP.

Fastest test: open “Preview the 30 Tools” first. If those forms address problems you actually have, then download the complete system. If they do not, skip it.

01

Choose one pressure point

Start with the repeated problem that is costing the restaurant the most in money, time, safety, guest trust, employee stability, or management attention.

02

Assign one accountable owner

Name the owner or manager responsible for the system, the review schedule, the source records, and the follow-up. Shared responsibility without a clear owner usually becomes no responsibility.

03

Start with 3–5 tools

Do not launch all 30 tools at once. Select the few tools that address the immediate problem and use them consistently through the chosen Accelerated or Guided first phase.

04

Teach before requiring

Use the manual and training deck to explain the purpose, standard, owner, frequency, and correct completion method before expecting managers or employees to use the forms.

05

Record, review, and decide

Use the forms for field evidence and the digital workbook for the live management view. Review results on the stated daily, weekly, monthly, or event-based schedule and assign a decision or corrective action.

06

Verify, then expand

Confirm that the correction actually changed the result. Keep completed records, close open actions with evidence, then add the next tools or the next system only when the current control is stable.

Which file should I use?

  • Manual DOCX: controlled local customization when approved changes are needed.
  • Manual PDF: stable reading, printing, and normal distribution copy.
  • Forms DOCX: editable master for approved local fields or controlled revisions.
  • Forms PDF: fixed print-ready forms for binders, meetings, and field use.
  • Digital XLSX: live control center for approved inputs, calculations, checks, dashboards, and action tracking.
  • Workbook PDF: fixed print/share version of the digital control structure.
  • Training PPTX: editable manager-training presentation.
  • Training PDF: fixed presentation/handout version for sharing and reference.
  • Complete ZIP: keep the full package together so the manual, forms, workbook, training, and guide stay connected.
Simple 30-day start

Install the system in a controlled sequence.

Days 1–5: Read the manual, identify the pressure point, name the system owner, confirm the source records, and choose the first 3–5 tools.

Days 6–10: Customize only what the restaurant genuinely needs, preserve one clean master copy, train the people who will complete or review the records, and confirm where completed evidence will be stored.

Days 11–30: Use the tools during real operations. Review the workbook and forms on schedule, identify missed standards or repeated patterns, assign corrective actions, and verify closure with evidence.

At day 30: Decide what to keep, revise, retire, or expand. Add more tools only when the first controls are being completed and reviewed reliably.

Important: Gravity tools support management control. They do not replace the restaurant's official accounting, payroll, POS, health-department, legal, tax, insurance, employment, safety, or other authoritative records and requirements. Use the current applicable authority whenever a rule or threshold is controlled outside the restaurant.

How the controls work

Control food and labor costs with evidence, not guesswork.

Gravity connects operating knowledge with forms, workbooks, and dedicated tools. It does not need to replace your existing POS or accounting software. Results depend on accurate records, practical decisions, and consistent manager follow-through.

01 · Food cost & inventory

Compare expected usage with actual results.

Use sales and recipe standards to estimate what the restaurant should have used. Compare that estimate with purchases, physical counts, transfers, and recorded waste to investigate the difference.

  1. Record: Verify invoices, receiving, waste, and inventory counts.
  2. Compare: Review theoretical versus actual cost and usage where reliable input data is available.
  3. Investigate: Check portions, pricing, count accuracy, receiving mistakes, and unrecorded movement.
  4. Verify: Apply one correction and compare subsequent periods.

Important: A variance is evidence of a discrepancy—not proof of theft or an automatically identified cause.

Explore the Food Cost system → Open Inventory Center

02 · Labor cost & scheduling

Budget labor before publishing the schedule.

Start with a realistic sales forecast, target labor dollars, average wage assumptions, and service requirements. Review planned staffing and overtime exposure, then compare the schedule with actual results.

  1. Forecast: Estimate demand using recent sales and known local events.
  2. Plan: Set a labor budget and coverage standards before assigning shifts.
  3. Review: Check hours, roles, skill coverage, overtime risk, and expected service levels.
  4. Improve: Use separate work-study observations to investigate bottlenecks and task duration.

Important: Forecasts and budgets guide decisions; they cannot guarantee exact staffing needs or savings. Shift Lab and Work Study Lab are separate tools.
