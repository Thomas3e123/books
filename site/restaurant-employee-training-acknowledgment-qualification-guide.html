Restaurant Management Guide · Definitions & records

Training vs. Acknowledgment vs. Qualification: A Restaurant Manager’s Guide

Use precise definitions so employee records do not claim more than the restaurant has actually verified.

Use precise definitions so employee records do not claim more than the restaurant has actually verified.

Written by Thomas Butler · Founder & Author, Thomas Monroe Books

Built from restaurant operating practice and the current controls inside The Gravity Restaurant Leadership System.

Reviewed October 9, 2026.

Three records that should not mean the same thing

  • Acknowledgment: confirmation that the employee received or reviewed required material.
  • Training completed: confirmation that the required instructional activity was completed.
  • Qualified or certified: the restaurant determined the employee met its defined performance requirements for the work.

Why the distinction matters

  • If every checkbox is called certified, the record becomes misleading. A person may read a fryer SOP without being ready to operate the fryer alone.
  • Clear definitions protect managers too: they show what was actually taught, observed, acknowledged, or approved instead of relying on vague status labels.

Cross-station learning

  • Employees should be able to explore published procedures outside their assigned station when the restaurant allows it. Viewing another station's SOP does not create assignment, training completion, or qualification.
  • If the employee later requests cross-training, move that interest into a structured training path rather than retroactively treating browsing as training.

A simple manager rule

  • Record only the level you can prove. If the restaurant has proof of review, record review. If it has proof of training, record training. If it has observed performance against a qualification standard, record qualification.
Gravity rule: Use the smallest control that makes the truth easier to see and the work easier to repeat. Records should describe what actually happened, not what the restaurant hopes happened.
