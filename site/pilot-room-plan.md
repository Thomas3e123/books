# Gravity 50-Person Pilot — Operator Playbook

**Purpose:** Put Gravity in front of real restaurant people, learn from actual use, and make verified improvements. A test click is useful product feedback; it is not proof that a restaurant result improved.

**Recruitment target:** 50 pilot contributors. This is a target, not a promise that all 50 will be restaurant operators. Invite a mix of restaurant leaders who can test a tool and operators who can try a 30-day or 60-day change in a real operation.

## What the pilot needs

- The public Gravity Pilot Room: `https://gravityleadershipsystem.com/pilot-room`
- The private manager dashboard: `https://gravityleadershipsystem.com/pilot-room-manager` (manager passkey required)
- One person to coordinate each participant and reply to questions.
- A phone or computer, internet access, and a current browser.
- Fictional restaurant details for software-only tests. Restaurant pilot participants should keep exact financial figures and private employee/guest records in their own systems.
- A simple outreach list showing who was invited, whether they replied, and whether they started. Do not add sensitive contact notes.

## Two participation tracks

| Track | Who it fits | What they do | What counts as evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tool and process tester | A restaurant leader, manager, or invited helper who can try a Gravity page, tool, download, or workflow | Choose a guide in the Pilot Room, follow its setup and cases, and mark each Pass, Fail, Blocked, Not tested, or Not applicable | The named case result, tool/page, device/browser, and a short note describing what happened |
| Restaurant improvement pilot | An owner or manager willing to install one small change in an operating restaurant | Choose one recurring problem, set a baseline in the restaurant’s own records, select three to five relevant Gravity resources, assign an owner, and use a 30-day or 60-day pace | Weekly evidence source, direction of change, blockers, owner and next action; exact measures stay with the restaurant |

A participant may do both tracks. Keep software usability findings separate from restaurant outcome claims.

## Step 1 — Invite people

1. Start with restaurant owners, general managers, and managers who can make or influence a small operating change.
2. Send the invitation and link them directly to `/pilot-room`.
3. Explain that the pilot asks for honest results, including “no change” or “this did not work.” Do not promise a specific business result.
4. Record invite date and source, then follow up once if there is no response.
5. Do not send bulk messages to a group or directory unless its rules allow it. Ask an administrator before posting in a private group.

**Track separately:** invitation sent → reply → room visit → enrollment. Do not infer enrollment from a link click.

## Step 2 — Enroll and orient

1. The participant enters their name, email, role, optional restaurant type, and one issue area.
2. They read the privacy note and agree to pilot contact and storage before creating a private record.
3. The room displays a private return link. The participant copies or bookmarks it and keeps it private.
4. Tell the participant not to share that link. It opens their own pilot record.
5. The participant chooses **Test a Gravity tool** or **Build my plan**.

## Step 3A — Run a software or process test

1. Choose one test protocol. Begin with a single tool rather than testing everything in one sitting.
2. Read the purpose and sample setup. Open the tool in another tab.
3. Use fictional restaurant data only. Never enter real payroll, bank/payment details, employee files, guest names, passwords, or exact restaurant financial records into a test report.
4. Follow each numbered case exactly as written. Record Pass only if the stated expected result occurred. Record Fail if the tool behaved differently. Record Blocked if the tool/page was unavailable or the case could not be completed. Use Not tested or Not applicable when appropriate.
5. Add the device/browser and a short note about the most important result. Save the report.
6. For a failure, go to **Report a problem**. Include the page/tool, what you expected, what happened, and the steps needed to repeat it. Avoid private restaurant data.
7. The coordinator reviews the manager dashboard, replies if clarification is needed, and records the issue as open, fixed, or still unverified.

**Inventory Center sample setup:** Use a disposable fictional restaurant called Pilot Kitchen. Create Canola Oil (bottle; case of 12; fictional cost $36/case; starting count two cases/24 bottles), Mozzarella (pound; case of 10 pounds; fictional cost $42/case; starting count 30 pounds), and Tomato Sauce (can; case of 6; fictional cost $24/case; starting count three cases/18 cans). If available, receive one oil case (+12 bottles), record two bottles as waste, then count 34 bottles. Check how the tool handles units, history, variance, and any available export or backup. If the published tool does not offer a step, mark it Blocked or Not applicable; do not invent a feature. Use a disposable copy or backup before destructive edits.

## Step 3B — Run a restaurant improvement pilot

1. Select one repeated pressure point that can be observed weekly. Avoid trying to fix several unrelated systems at once.
2. Describe what keeps happening in observable terms. Assign one accountable role or person.
3. Pick a measure and its source. Record the exact starting value only in the restaurant’s own records; the Pilot Room stores the measure name and whether a baseline was recorded, not its exact value.
4. Choose three to five resources that directly support this problem. Do not install every Gravity tool.
5. Choose the **30-day accelerated** or **60-day guided** pace. Use the dates generated by the room as review reminders.
6. Train and practice the standard before enforcing it. Name who demonstrates, who practices, and who verifies.
7. Submit a weekly check-in: what was completed, where evidence can be checked, whether the measure improved/held/worsened, what blocked progress, and the owner/date for the next action.
8. At midpoint, choose **continue**, **correct**, or **stop and investigate**. Explain why. Do not keep a change that creates a safety, legal, guest, or employee problem.
9. At final review, compare the result with the starting baseline in the restaurant’s own records. Record whether the standard held and what should happen next. Report negative or mixed results honestly.

## Step 4 — Manager follow-up

1. Open the manager dashboard and review new enrollments, plans, test reports, check-ins, issue reports, and messages.
2. Acknowledge a new participant and clarify any missing detail.
3. Assign each issue an owner and next action outside the participant’s report. Use the manager notes for non-sensitive tracking only.
4. When a change is said to be fixed, verify it on the relevant page/tool and record the evidence. Until then leave it unverified.
5. Keep restaurant outcome evidence distinct from tool usability feedback and marketing activity.
6. Review participation weekly: invitations, replies, enrollments, plans saved, tests started, cases completed, blocked cases, first check-ins, midpoints, finals, and unanswered issues.

## Step 5 — Reward record

The pilot manager dashboard includes the qualifying-contribution and award fields so the same approved reward standard can be applied across tool testing and restaurant pilot work. Mark a contribution qualifying only when the participant’s evidence resulted in a material, verified improvement to Gravity. Record when any promised access or final relevant version is actually delivered. Do not count an enrollment or unverified suggestion as a qualifying contribution.

## What counts as a useful pilot outcome

- A person can explain Gravity’s purpose and find the next step without coaching.
- A tool works as its instructions say, or a reproducible defect is recorded.
- A real operator completes the planned installation and weekly reviews.
- A restaurant measure changes in a documented direction and the evidence source is identified.
- The standard continues to hold after the active pilot period, or the reason it did not is recorded.

A low response rate is still a finding. It means the invitation, audience, timing, or offer needs attention; it does not prove the product works or fails.

## What the Gravity coordinator must do personally

- Contact and invite real operators or community administrators; the room cannot recruit them by itself.
- Answer participant questions and keep the manager dashboard current.
- Confirm any claimed restaurant result with the participant using their own records; do not request sensitive records through this site.
- Verify any claimed Gravity repair on the relevant live page or build before marking it fixed.
- Decide whether feedback meets the approved reward standard and deliver that reward.
- Review privacy, analytics, and production event reporting with the account owner; a participant cannot verify GA4 event receipt without account access.

## Test catalog inside the Pilot Room

The room includes step-by-step guides for the publicly linked Gravity Assist workspaces: Forms Personalizer, Document Studio, Shift Lab, SOP Foundry and hosted employee materials, Numbers Lab, Work Study Lab, Inventory Center, Recipe Works, Handbook Creator, and Gravity Assist; the QR Code Maker and the eleven other Quick Tools; Gravity Aware; Ask Gravity; books, registration, downloads, and Book Reader; Windows programs; saving/export/backup/recovery; support/contact; privacy/accessibility/security; Analytics readiness; visitor-to-pilot conversion; recruitment/enrollment; and pilot follow-up.

If a tool is missing from the published entry point, mark its guide Blocked and stop. The purpose is to reveal what is ready for real use, not to pretend an unfinished tool is available.
