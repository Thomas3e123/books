## The problem
Labor percentage rises. Cutting hours without investigating the cause may lower scheduled wages while creating delays, overtime, missed prep, safety risks or guest problems.

## Three things to remember
1. **A percentage is a signal, not a diagnosis.** Lower sales can raise labor percentage even when labor dollars do not change.
2. **Not all hours are interchangeable.** Opening, closing, prep, required checks and skilled station coverage must still happen.
3. **Savings must survive a service check.** A lower labor figure is not a valid improvement if late prep, guest waits, overtime or unfinished work increase.

## What to notice before changing the schedule
- Compare forecast sales with actual sales for the same daypart.
- Compare planned hours, worked hours, wage costs and overtime on the same basis.
- Identify required fixed work and the person qualified to complete each task.
- Review start times, gaps in skill coverage, breaks, preparation readiness, and closing workload.
- Account for applicable wage, break, overtime and scheduling requirements.

Compare several similar shifts, not one convenient result. Record the actual labor dollars and percentage **alongside** prep completion, guest wait/service measures, overtime, guest complaints, and closing completion. Use consistent payroll-burden assumptions.
